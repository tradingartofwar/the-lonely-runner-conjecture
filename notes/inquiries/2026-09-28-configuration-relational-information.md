# Configuration-level relational information

## The idea to carry forward

Some facts concern relationships among parts, rather than properties of any one part. If Jon and Sam are each booked Monday, Wednesday and Friday, the statement that their schedules coincide is a fact about their comparison. It is determined by the two complete schedules; it is not additional independently supplied data.

The Lonely Runner counterpart is the joint trajectory

`(v_1 t,...,v_k t) mod 1`

under a common clock and common start. Speeds, blocking fractions, meeting counts and pairwise overlap totals can be useful summaries while hiding decisive joint restrictions.

Two questions from the brief should remain explicit:

> What information exists only in the relationships among the runners, and is therefore invisible when we summarize runners or pairs separately?

> What configuration-level relational invariant or constraint distinguishes complete-looking blocking schedules from ones that must leave a lonely moment?

The central methodological statement is:

> The missing information may not be absent from the mathematics; it may be absent from our representation.

“Relational” here describes what the fact is about and which summaries expose it. It does not mean the full speed vector is mathematically insufficient, or that the runners causally influence one another.

## Candidate structures

- Relative phase alignment and its common-start constants.
- Placement of overlap inside openings left by other runners.
- Repeated timing relationships across laps and windows.
- Simultaneous or higher-order blocking.
- Compatibility of several schedules at the same time.
- Integer or residue relations among multiple speeds.
- Direction and timing of entering or leaving safety at equality contacts.
- Constraints on a whole configuration that cannot be assigned to one runner alone.

An invariant must specify its transformation group: reflection, speed scaling, time translation, relabelling or a particular deformation. A useful relational constraint need not be an invariant. Do not call every new statistic an invariant.
