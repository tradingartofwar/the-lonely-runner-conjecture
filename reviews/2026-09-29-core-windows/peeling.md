The main deduction is a sufficient condition **b >= 48**, after the inherited
condition has already reduced the smallest residual speed to a <= 34.
Thus any configuration not certified by these two tests has

`a in {2,3,6,...,34}, a < b <= 47`,

while c and d remain unbounded. This is not a finite configuration reduction,
an all-reference claim, or a full Lonely Runner proof.

## 1. Scope and inherited inputs

There are n=8 common-start runners, with selected reference 0 and distinct
positive integer speeds

`1,4,5,a,b,c,d`, where `a<b<c<d`

and a,b,c,d exclude 1,4,5. Equality at distance 1/8 is safe. A speed v blocks
the open intervals

`((m-1/8)/v,(m+1/8)/v)`.

Its blocked width is `1/(4v)` and each closed safe gap has width `3/(4v)`.

The inherited short-kernel argument gives a point safe for the four
residuals in every closed interval of length

`H = 1/a + (3/4)(1/b+1/c+1/d)`.

The fixed core {1,4,5} is safe throughout `J=[9/32,3/8]`, whose width is
`L=3/32`. In particular a>=35 already suffices. The present argument handles
the remaining 31 possible values of a by absorbing a into the core and
using the three-residual span budget proved below.

## 2. Core geometry and a measure-only baseline

Direct intersection of the three threshold conditions gives the six closed
components of the {1,4,5}-safe set in [0,1]:

| Component | Width |
| --- | --- |
| `[1/8,7/40]` | `1/20` |
| `[9/32,3/8]` | `3/32` |
| `[17/40,15/32]` | `7/160` |
| `[17/32,23/40]` | `7/160` |
| `[5/8,23/32]` | `3/32` |
| `[33/40,7/8]` | `1/20` |

Their total measure is 3/8. An integer speed a blocks measure exactly 1/4
over [0,1], so the four-core safe set has measure at least 1/8, for every
positive integer a. This is a stronger uniform positivity observation than
using only the common overlap near t=0.

For completeness, a crude component bound is a+5. Before adding a there are
six components. Its a-1 interior blocked intervals can each increase the
component count by at most one. The two endpoint blocked intervals cannot
split an existing component. Thus a<=34 would give a positive component of
width at least `1/(8*39)=1/312`. Isolated components do not invalidate this
pigeonhole argument, since they contribute zero measure. The next lemma
gives a much stronger bound without counting components.

## 3. One-interval clipping lemma

Consider a periodic open blocked train of interval width B, separated by
closed safe gaps of width S. In any closed interval I of width L>B there is
a closed safe subinterval of width at least

`min(S,(L-B)/2)`.                                              (1)

Proof. If I contains an entire safe gap, its width S gives the result.
Otherwise I can meet at most one blocked occurrence: meeting two distinct
occurrences would contain the entire intervening safe gap. Consequently at
most B of I is blocked and the safe part has at most two components. One has
width at least `(L-B)/2`. Contacts at open blocker endpoints are safe and do
not weaken the argument. This also explains why the conclusion concerns a
closed safe interval, not merely positive measure.

Apply (1) to speed a on J. For a>=3 the hypothesis L>B holds, and it yields

`width(W_a) >= min(3/(4a), 3/64-1/(8a))`.                    (2)

The second term increases with a; the first decreases. Therefore for every
integer `6<=a<=34`,

`width(W_a) >= min(3/136,5/192)=3/136`.

For a=2, the whole J is a-safe. For a=3, `[1/8,7/40]` is a-safe. Hence
every remaining a has a four-core-safe window of width at least 3/136.
This uniform width is sharp when a ranges up to 34: no interval safe for
speed 34 alone can be wider than `3/(4*34)=3/136`.

This already implies the simple cutoff b>=78: monotonicity and distinctness
give

`1/(4b)+1/(2c)+1/d <= 1/312+1/158+1/80 < 3/136`.

The next section combines (2) with the inherited H test to improve 78 to 48.

## 4. Three-residual span budget

For positive speeds b<c<d, set

`T_3=1/(4b)+1/(2c)+1/d`.                                    (3)

Every closed interval of length at least T_3 contains a common-safe point
for these three speeds, even if their phases are arbitrary.

Here is the chain argument with multiplicities made explicit. Write the
periods as `P=1/b >= q=1/c >= r=1/d`. Selected blocking occurrences have
strictly increasing right endpoints, and every old right endpoint lies
strictly inside the next open occurrence. A transition into a period-p
occurrence advances the right endpoint by strictly less than p/4.

In a q,r chain, q cannot repeat. Between its first return there would be
only one r occurrence: consecutive equal-label occurrences cannot overlap.
The return would advance by less than `(q+r)/4<=q/2`, yet two q right
endpoints differ by at least q. Thus a q,r chain contains at most one q and
two r occurrences.

In a P,q,r chain, P cannot repeat. Between its first return, the q,r piece
has at most one q and two r occurrences. The return advances by less than
`(P+q+2r)/4<=P`, whereas two P right endpoints differ by at least P.
Removing the unique P, if present, leaves at most two q,r pieces. Thus the
whole chain has at most one P, two q, and four r occurrences. Its connected
open union has width strictly less than

`P/4+2q/4+4r/4=T_3`.

For a chain with multiple occurrences, strict overlaps give the strict
inequality; a single occurrence is also strictly shorter than T_3. An open
blocking cover of a closed interval of length T_3 would generate a finite
strict chain extending beyond both ends, which is impossible. Local
finiteness follows from positive periods. Thus equality in the window
length test is allowed; the selected safe point need not have a positive
safe neighborhood for the three remaining constraints.

## 5. The analytic cutoff b >= 48

Suppose b>=48. Distinct ordered integers give c>=49 and d>=50, so

`T_3 <= 1/192+1/98+1/50 = 8329/235200 < 25/704`.              (4)

The last inequality follows by cross multiplication:
`8329*704=5863616 < 5880000=25*235200`.

There are three analytically prescribed ranges for a.

**Range A: a>=22.** The original four-residual test already succeeds:

`H <= 1/22+9/(4*48) = 65/704 < 66/704 = 3/32`.

This step uses only b,c,d>=48, so strict distinctness is unnecessary here.

**Range B: 11<=a<=21.** Formula (2) gives

`width(W_a) >= min(3/(4*21),3/64-1/(8*11))`

`             = min(1/28,25/704) = 25/704`.

Together with (4), the three-residual budget certifies a selected-reference
safe point inside W_a.

**Range C: a in {2,3,6,7,8,9,10}.** The following seven explicit intervals
complete the argument; this table is an analytic certificate list, not the
output of an enumeration.

| a | Four-core-safe interval | Width | Range of a*t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | `[9/32,3/8]` | `3/32` | `[9/16,3/4]` |
| 3 | `[1/8,7/40]` | `1/20` | `[3/8,21/40]` |
| 6 | `[17/40,15/32]` | `7/160` | `[51/20,45/16]` |

Each listed interval lies in one of the six fixed-core components from

Every listed width is at least `7/160>25/704`. This completes all 31 values
of a without evaluating a one-parameter table, let alone b,c,d tuples.

## 6. Limits of the two tests

The numerical cutoff 48 cannot be reduced to 47 using only these two width
tests, even if the four-core component widths are computed optimally.
Consider the explicitly supplied tuple

`(a,b,c,d)=(21,47,48,49)`.

Its inherited four-residual budget satisfies

`H-3/32 = 335/442176 > 0`.

Any interval safe for speed 21 has width at most `1/28`, while

`T_3-1/28 = 95/221088 > 0`.

Thus neither H on J nor T_3 on any component of the four-core safe set can
certify this tuple. This is an analytically constructed limitation of the
two sufficient tests. It is not a failure of loneliness, and no threshold
oracle or time scan was run on the tuple.

The new relational information is the placement of a safe gap of runner a
inside an actual fixed-core component. The global measure argument confirms
that four-core safe time is plentiful; the clipping lemma turns one known
core interval and the maximum blocked-run width into a guaranteed contiguous
window. The inherited chain multiplicities then convert that concrete
window into a second-speed cutoff.

Remaining scope: a<=34 and b<=47 still leave two unbounded speeds. Absorbing
b would create five core constraints with total nominal blocked duty 5/4;
the elementary quarter-duty measure argument no longer forces their safe
set to have positive measure. No assertion resolving that next obstacle is
made here.
