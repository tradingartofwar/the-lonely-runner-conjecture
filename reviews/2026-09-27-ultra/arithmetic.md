## 1. General rational-partition aliasing lemma

Fix `0<δ<1/2`, put `p=2δ`, and write

\[
 b_\delta(x)=\mathbf1_{\{\|x\|<\delta\}},\qquad
 C_\delta(z)=p\lfloor z+\delta\rfloor+
                 \min(\{z+\delta\},p),\qquad
 \psi_\delta(z)=C_\delta(z)-pz.
\]

The primitive is continuous, has derivative `bδ` away from threshold points, and `ψδ` is one-periodic. Direct substitution gives

\[
 \psi_\delta(0)=\psi_\delta(1/2)=\delta. \tag{1}
\]

Let `P` be an even positive integer, and let a finite measurable partition of an interval `I` have all its boundaries in `(1/P)Z`. Endpoints and isolated atoms may be retained separately; they have zero Lebesgue mass. For every positive integer `h`, set `y=Ph/2`. On every partition cell `[a,b]`,

\[
 \int_a^b b_\delta(yt)\,dt
 =p(b-a)+\frac{\psi_\delta(yb)-\psi_\delta(ya)}y
 =p(b-a). \tag{2}
\]

Both endpoint phases are integers or half-integers, which proves the second equality. Summing proves (2) for every union of partition cells, hence for every fixed exact-state region or fixed joint-blocking region whose boundaries lie on that grid.

**Consequences.** Every moment involving the variable blocker is `p` times the corresponding fixed-only moment. Every fixed state of mass `m` splits into masses `(1-p)m` and `pm`. This establishes actual independence of the **binary variable-blocker observable** from the finite vector of fixed blocking observables when time is uniform on `I`. It does not establish independence of the full phase coordinates, trajectories, temporal schedules, or different samples in time.

There is also an exact maximality statement for the full grid. Piecewise linearity gives

\[
 \{x\bmod1:\psi_\delta(x)=\delta\}=\{0,1/2\}. \tag{3}
\]

If the variable blocker occupies proportion `p` in **every one** of the `P` grid cells of `[0,1]`, its correction at consecutive grid points is constant. Starting at zero, all phases in the cyclic subgroup generated by `y/P` must therefore lie in the two-point set in (3). Thus

\[
 \text{neutral on every grid cell}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\mid 2y.
 \tag{4}
\]

For odd `P`, this forces `P|y` and all grid endpoints have phase zero. For even `P`, the additional half-period class provides the endpoint alias. This maximality statement concerns the *whole grid*, not a coarser selected collection of regions; other cancellations can occur for coarser data.

## 2. A threshold-denominator and 2-adic construction

Let the fixed positive integer speeds be `V={v1,...,vm}`, and let

\[
 \delta=a/d\in(0,1/2),\qquad (a,d)=1,\qquad d\text{ even},\qquad
 L=\operatorname{lcm}(V).
\]

Keep this threshold fixed throughout the statement. It is not automatically the Lonely Runner threshold after changing the number of runners.

Every fixed threshold event has the form

\[
 t=(k\pm a/d)/v=(dk\pm a)/(dv),
\]

so the complete fixed-state partition of `[0,1]` lies on the grid `P=dL`. Set

\[
 y_h=(dL/2)h. \tag{5}
\]

The partition lemma gives identical every-order full-period moments for all `h`. In addition `v|y_h` for every fixed speed, so `gcd(v,y_h)=v` for all `h`; the entire labelled fixed-fixed/variable-fixed gcd table is unchanged. Exclude any finitely many `h` causing duplicate speeds if distinct runners are required.

At a threshold event controlled by speed `v`,

\[
 y_h t=\frac{h(L/v)(dk\pm a)}2.
\]

Since `d` is even and `a` is odd, `dk±a` is odd. Therefore:

* even `h` puts the variable runner at phase zero at **every fixed threshold event**;
* odd `h` puts it at phase `1/2` exactly when `L/v` is odd, equivalently when `ν₂(v)=ν₂(L)`;
* the other fixed threshold events have phase zero also for odd `h`.

The apparent reference to a particular controller is unambiguous. A threshold time controlled by `v` has reduced denominator with 2-adic valuation `ν₂(d)+ν₂(v)`, because its numerator is odd. Two speeds controlling the same rational threshold time must consequently have the same `ν₂(v)`.

**Conditional existence construction.** Suppose a nondegenerate rational window `J`, with endpoints on the same grid, has a finite fixed-runner allowed set `T`, and every member of `T` is a fixed threshold event. Let `Tmax` be the members whose threshold controllers have maximal `ν₂(v)` among `V`. Then

\[
 F_J(y_h)=
 \begin{cases}
 T_{\max},&h\text{ odd},\\
 \varnothing,&h\text{ even}.
 \end{cases} \tag{6}
\]

Indeed the fixed runners already eliminate `J\T`, and the variable runner is either strictly blocked at phase zero or strictly safe at phase one-half on `T`. If `Tmax` is nonempty, (6) gives an infinite family with identical local and full-period moments, identical gcds, and different local existence. This is a general sufficient template; it does not assert that an appropriate fixed configuration/window exists for every threshold or runner count.

For the present fixed speeds `(1,3,4,5,10,28)`, `L=420`, `d=8`, and (5) is `y=1680h`. At `9/32`, controllers `4` and `28` both have maximal valuation `ν₂=2`; at `3/8`, controller `3` has valuation zero. This identifies the parity distinction without reconstructing contact geometry.

## 5. Finite decision criterion for endpoint identifiability

The residue-classification method in the existing notes has a general formulation. Fix finitely many rational interval unions `A1,...,As`, a rational threshold, and finitely many rational candidate contacts. Choose `P` divisible by every interval-endpoint and contact denominator. The data

\[
 M_j(y)=|B_y\cap A_j|
\]

include any desired fixed-runner intersection or state moments. There is a common positive integer `D` and a finite integer-vector table `e(r)`, `0≤r<P`, such that

\[
 M(y)=M_\infty+\frac{e(y\bmod P)}{Dy},\qquad
 (M_\infty)_j=2\delta|A_j|. \tag{9}
\]

This follows directly by summing primitive endpoint corrections. Any pointwise endpoint-safety label `f(y)` is a finite residue label `f(r)` after enlarging `P` to include its rational points. Local nonemptiness is such a label when the fixed allowed set is a finite contact set.

Here is a necessary and sufficient finite test for exact identifiability of `f` from `M`:

1. All residues with `e(r)=0` must have the same label; they form one common summary class.
2. For nonzero vectors, write `e(r)=g_r u_r`, where `g_r>0` is the gcd of its coordinates and `u_r` is the signed primitive vector. Only equal `u_r=u_s` can collide at positive speeds.
3. For such a pair, reduce `g_r:g_s` to coprime `a:b`. A collision is possible exactly when `br≡as (mod P)`. If possible, its speeds are `y=ak,z=bk`, where `ak≡r,bk≡s (mod P)`. Require `f(r)=f(s)` for every admitted pair.

For completeness, if `ua+vb=1`, the displayed congruence is sufficient because `k=ur+vs (mod P)` solves both equations. Each admitted class has arbitrarily large positive solutions, so finitely many forbidden duplicate speeds do not remove a genuine collision family. Unequal signed primitive vectors or the zero/nonzero distinction cannot collide. These observations prove necessity and sufficiency, rather than merely furnishing a bounded search.

Fixed-speed gcd annotations can be included by enlarging `P` to be divisible by those speeds and retaining only matches with the same `gcd(v,r)`. This test concerns exact rational data. It gives no uniform bound on how many residues or rational bits are needed as the fixed configuration varies.

**Uniform precision obstruction.** If the endpoint label has at least two residue values realized by admissible speeds, no finite fixed duration suite of this kind has a positive uniform absolute-error margin between the two labels. On each residue progression, (9) tends to the same `M∞` as `y→∞`. Indeed for a union of `N` intervals,

\[
 \bigl||B_y\cap A|-2\delta|A|\bigr|
 \leq \frac{N(2\delta)(1-2\delta)}y, \tag{10}
\]

because the range of `ψδ` has width `(2δ)(1-2δ)`. This approximation statement does not need rational endpoints. It is separate from exact collisions, and explains why the successful probe (8) still has vanishing separation. If extra discrete annotations are retained, apply this precision conclusion within an annotation class containing both labels; one cannot assume such a class exists automatically.

## 6. What the orbit and Fourier viewpoints retain

For the current family, introduce the auxiliary map

\[
 (t,x)\mapsto(t,3t,4t,5t,10t,28t,x)\pmod1.
\]

Its image is a two-dimensional torus. The physical common-start trajectory is the one-dimensional subtorus `x=yt`. Equations (2) and (7) say that for this finite Boolean partition, the physical occupation law equals the auxiliary product occupation law exactly. They do **not** say the physical measure equals two-dimensional Haar measure: the coordinate relation `x=yt` holds identically, and the speed-one phase already determines every other phase.

The earlier affine argument uses an open auxiliary safe region plus a quantitative rounding step to reach it from `x=qt`. That is compatible with this example. Above the local window `J`, the fixed opening is only two time fibers, which have two-dimensional measure zero. Matching occupation laws cannot decide whether the arithmetic graph hits their safe pieces. At `t=9/32`, that intersection is exactly the congruence `945h/2 mod1`, giving parity. There is no positive slow-time margin for the previous strict-point rounding argument to use.

In Fourier terms, a joint duration is only the zero temporal Fourier coefficient of its joint-blocking indicator. All joint durations retain the full *state histogram*, but not the nonzero temporal coefficients of those states. Even a single runner illustrates the missing information:

\[
 \int_0^1 b_\delta(yt)e^{-2\pi iyt}\,dt
 =\frac{\sin(2\pi\delta)}\pi\ne0,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1 b_\delta(2yt)e^{-2\pi iyt}\,dt=0. \tag{11}
\]

The second identity follows from frequency support on multiples of `2y`. This separates the anchor speeds by a chosen nonzero frequency, while their complete occupation histograms agree. It is not a claim that one fixed frequency works at all unbounded speeds. Pair gcds encode repetition periods but do not encode these full frequency supports or the integer relation `x=yt`.

An entirely rational chronology check is also archived. On the first grid cell `[0,1/3360]`, both blockers occupy duration `1/(4·3360)`, but

\[
 \int_0^{1/3360}t\,b_{1/8}(1680t)\,dt=\frac1{32\cdot3360^2},\qquad
 \int_0^{1/3360}t\,b_{1/8}(3360t)\,dt=\frac1{8\cdot3360^2}.
\]

Weighting by time preserves a distinction ordinary duration erased. A full-period first time moment is a poor substitute: common-start integer blocking products are symmetric under `t↦1-t`, forcing that particular weighted moment to be half their ordinary duration.
