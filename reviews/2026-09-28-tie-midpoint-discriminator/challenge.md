## Exact semantics and what the score measures

For every positive base-pair component `I=(l,r)`, the implementation samples the exact midpoint `(l+r)/2`. For each complement speed `v` it computes

`distance(v t, Z) - 1/8`

with `fractions.Fraction`. Blocking is strict distance `<1/8`; equality is safe. Consequently a zero score means threshold-safe midpoint contact, not blocking. “Nonnegative predicts containment” in the result is only a diagnostic classification; it is not an implication.

Full containment necessarily makes every prescribed midpoint nonblocking, so a negative midpoint score would refute containment. The converse fails in the frozen development data. For `squares:1,2,5:12`:

- successful `{25,121}` scores `1475/13068`;
- failed `{25,169}` scores `23/1352 > 0`;
- speed 49 nevertheless blocks the latter on `(79/392,21/104)`.

The reflected window has the reflected positive violation. These are the two recorded score-sign false positives. They are the central preserved counterexample, not a nuisance to average away.

Maximizing the minimum margin is a coherent frozen direction: it favors the pair whose worst sampled complement phase is farther from the blocking threshold. But it ignores component width and the complement phase slope to either endpoint. A complement can therefore be safe at the midpoint and cross into blocking near a component edge, exactly as the counterexample does. No normalization or endpoint sample may be added after seeing this result without opening a new investigation.

All sixteen tied pair-label instances in the evaluated scope have exactly one positive component. The experiment therefore does not test the advertised minimum aggregation across multiple components; it only tests the minimum over two complement speeds at one midpoint per tied pair. Claims about multi-component maximin behavior would be unsupported.
