## 1. Every prescribed full itinerary is a linear system

Put `delta=1/8`, `p_i=1/v_i`, and `beta_i=-alpha_i/v_i`. The blocked
occurrence around the integer meeting label `m` is the open interval

`I(i,m)=(L(i,m),R(i,m))`,

`L(i,m)=(m-1/8)p_i+beta_i`,

`R(i,m)=(m+1/8)p_i+beta_i`.

In the physical common-start model all `beta_i=0`. Allowing arbitrary shifts
is an auxiliary model and must remain labeled as such. Period ordering for
`a<=b<=c<=d` is `p_a>=p_b>=p_c>=p_d>0`.

For a prescribed moving call into `(i,m)`, with current input `t`, the exact
conditions are

`0<R(i,m)-t<p_i/4`,

and its output is `R(i,m)`. Both inequalities are strict: either endpoint is
safe and does not move. For a prescribed nonmoving call using safe lap `m`,
the exact closed inequalities are

`R(i,m)<=t<=L(i,m+1)`.

After prescribing the call word, which calls move, their occurrence labels,
and the safe-lap labels at every nonmoving call, substitute the preceding
output for `t`. This yields a finite system of rational linear equalities and
strict/weak inequalities in the periods, shifts, and initial input. It is
necessary and sufficient for precisely that full scalar projection trace.
In the auxiliary phase model, scale and time-translation gauges can fix
`p_a=1` and one chosen endpoint at zero. For a physical common-start system,
a translation must carry the induced shifts explicitly; one cannot also
retain `beta_i=0` in the translated local coordinates. No optimizer is
needed to obtain the representation.

A moving word alone omits the nonmoving-call inequalities and therefore is
only a necessary chain certificate for a prescribed schedule. A strict chain
does not by itself establish an actual trajectory of
`a,b,c,d,c,d,b,c,d,c,d`. An open covering family omits still more information.

One immediate necessary inequality: if two selected endpoints belong to
runner `i` and their meeting labels differ by `r>=1`, summing the strict
overlap inequalities between them gives

`(4r-1)p_i < sum(period of each intervening selected occurrence)`.

All repetitions, including repeated colors, count in that sum.

## 2. General no-double-block lemma

This is the construction agent's useful generalization of the two direct
word obstructions below, independently checked here.

Let an increasing-right-endpoint chain have `2ell+1` selected occurrences,
with colors `i_0,...,i_(2ell)` and endpoints `x_0,...,x_(2ell)`. Suppose:

1. `i_(j+ell)=i_j` for `0<=j<=ell`;
2. matching occurrences in the two copies satisfy
   `x_(j+ell)=x_j+c_(i_j)`, where `c_i=h_i p_i` is independent of which
   occurrence of color `i` is matched;
3. every transition is a moving transition, so
   `g_j=p_(i_(j+1))/4-(x_(j+1)-x_j)>0`;
4. the integer label translation satisfies `h_i>=q_i`, where `q_i` is the
   number of appearances of color `i` in one cyclic block of `ell` edges.

Condition 4 follows automatically from strictly increasing selected labels
when two copies translate the complete occurrence pattern of each color:
the last selected label in the first copy precedes the first in the next.
Consecutive labels give `h_i=q_i`; skipped labels are allowed. Unused colors
have `q_i=h_i=c_i=0`.

Let `r=i_0=i_ell=i_(2ell)`. The overlap sum in the first block is

`S=sum_(j=0)^(ell-1) g_j = (1/4)sum_i q_i p_i-c_r`

`  <= (1/4)sum_i c_i-c_r`.

In the matching edge of the second block,

`g_(j+ell)=g_j+c_(i_j)-c_(i_(j+1))`.

Strict positivity in both copies therefore requires

`g_j>max(0,c_(i_(j+1))-c_(i_j))`.

Summing gives `S>V`, where `V` is the total positive variation of the `c`
values along the closed color walk. Following the walk from `r` to a color
attaining its maximum shows

`V>=max_used(c_i)-c_r`.

Since at most four colors are used and unused colors have `c_i=0`,

`max_used(c_i)-c_r >= (1/4)sum_i c_i-c_r >= S`.

This contradicts `S>V`. Hence no such doubled block is a strict chain.
The argument does not need speed ordering or common starts. The two copies
must include the closing edge twice: `2ell` edges, hence `2ell+1` selected
endpoints. Merely writing two copies of a finite color string can omit that
last needed transition.

**Scope limit:** repeated color words with changing, nonconstant matching
label increments need not meet condition 2. This is not yet a uniform bound
for all changing itineraries. The lemma is a necessary-chain exclusion, so
it applies to actual projection trajectories but does not manufacture one.

## 3. Analytically declared word patterns

Before any exact program check, the affine agent selected the inherited

### Inherited `A,D,B,C,D,A,D`

The preserved failure contains `A,D,B,C,D,A`; the next direct `D` extension
cannot repeat its same occurrence pattern. Write `p_a=A`, `p_d=D`, place the
first A endpoint at zero, and place the three consecutive D endpoints at
`x,x+D,x+2D`; the next A endpoint is `A`. The first D transition requires
`x<D/4`. The transition from the second D to A requires
`x+D>3A/4`. These force `D>3A/5`. The attempted next D transition requires
`x+2D<A+D/4`. Combined with `x+D>3A/4`, this forces `D<A/3`, a contradiction.
This uses only three necessary comparisons. It does not exclude changing
the moving word or skipping to a different intermediate runner.

### Tiling-motivated `A,C,D,B,C,D,A`

Here A returns after one occurrence period and C,D each appear twice with
consecutive occurrence labels. Summing overlap inequalities from the first
C to the second gives `3p_c<p_b+p_d`. Since `p_d<=p_c`, it follows that
`2p_c<p_b`. Summing the full A return gives

`3p_a<p_b+2p_c+2p_d<3p_b<=3p_a`,

which is impossible. Thus this particular cyclic word is already excluded
after one full cycle under the specified speed order. The general doubled
block lemma is useful because it does not require this order-specific
shortcut and also covers the other critical tilings.

## 4. Explicit neighborhoods of the three declared tilings

Use period-one templates, anchoring the first A right endpoint at zero:

| Word | Periods `(p_a,p_b,p_c,p_d)` | Shifts `(beta_a,beta_b,beta_c,beta_d)` | Minimum tile width `gamma` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ABCD` | `(1,1,1,1)` | `(-1/8,1/8,3/8,5/8)` | `1/4` |
| `ACDBCD` | `(1,1,1/2,1/2)` | `(-1/8,3/8,1/16,3/16)` | `1/8` |
| `ADBDCD` | `(1,1,1,1/3)` | `(-1/8,5/24,13/24,1/24)` | `1/12` |

In each template the blocked intervals tile by endpoint contact, without
interior overlap. These contacts themselves are safe and do not advance.

For one fixed template, put `rho=gamma/336`. Suppose each period and shift
differs from its listed value by less than `rho`. Then every period exceeds
`1/6` and is below `3/2`, and every shift has absolute value below 2. Any
blocked interval meeting `[-1,3]` has occurrence label `|m|<=40`: its center
lies within `[-1-p_i/8,3+p_i/8]`, and the bounds on `p_i,beta_i` give this
loose integer bound. For those labels each endpoint moves by less than

`(|m|+1/8+1)rho <= (41+1/8)rho < gamma/8`.

The gap between the right endpoint of a nominal tile and the left endpoint
of a tile at least two steps later is at least `gamma`. Consequently, after
perturbation, such a later interval cannot contain the current right
endpoint. Earlier intervals have smaller right endpoints. The same
occurrence is safe at its right endpoint. The **only possible moving
successor is therefore the next nominal tile**.

Starting at the perturbed A endpoint near zero, if `2ell` successive moving
transitions existed they would follow precisely two copies of the template
word and stay close to `[0,2]`. All candidate intervals during this reasoning
meet the buffered window `[-1,3]`, so the finite endpoint bounds apply. Their
labels are the consecutive nominal ones and satisfy the no-double-block
lemma, a contradiction. Thus a chain whose first selected moving endpoint
is this A occurrence has at most `2ell` selected moving occurrences:

- at most 8 for `ABCD`;
- at most 12 for `ACDBCD`;
- at most 12 for `ADBDCD`.

The same argument permits a cyclic rotation, label changes, and a common
translation of each anchored template. These are **local neighborhood
bounds**. They do not assert that every input lies in one of these explicit
neighborhoods or that 12 is a global bound. A global compactness argument
must separately justify its normalization, its limiting tiling
classification, and what happens when some normalized periods tend to zero.

## 6. Subcritical chain bounds and the vanishing-period boundary

The following supplementary proof candidates arose while checking the
remaining compactness issue. They concern arbitrary strict selected chains,
so they do not depend on the eleven-call scheduler.

For `k<=3` active colors let `G` be the sum of their centered blocking
primitives. As in the pinned termination note,

`G'=M-k/4`, and `osc(G)<=(3/16)sum_i p_i`.

Between the first and last selected endpoints a strict chain is blocked
almost everywhere, hence `M>=1`. If its endpoint span is `T`,

`T <= [3/(4(4-k))]sum_i p_i`

`  <= [3k/(4(4-k))]p_max`.

Occurrences of a largest-period color are at least `p_max` apart. For two
colors the span bound is `3p_max/4`, so that color occurs at most once.
Removing it leaves at most two one-color chains, each with at most one
occurrence. Thus **every two-color strict chain has at most three selected
occurrences**. For three colors the span bound is `9p_max/4`, so a
largest-period color occurs at most three times. Removing those occurrences
leaves at most four two-color chains. Thus **every three-color strict chain
has at most `3+4*3=15` selected occurrences**. These are sufficient bounds,
not claimed sharp. Endpoint equality in the time-span bound is harmless;
the numerical bounds are still strictly below one and three periods,
respectively.

Now suppose a sequence of four-color chains has periods normalized by
`p_max=1` and first selected endpoint translated to zero. Reduce each shift
modulo its period, which changes no blocked set. Along a subsequence the
periods and shifts converge. Suppose some limiting periods vanish. Let
`S` be the colors with positive limiting periods and `F` the others. Both
sets are nonempty, and each has at most three colors.

For one periodic blocked set of period `p`, its measure in any interval of
length `L` is at most `L/4+3p/16`, by the same primitive oscillation bound.
The union of the limiting slow blocked sets therefore has measure at most

`|S|+(3/16)sum_(i in S) p_i* <= 57/16 < 4`

inside `[0,4]`. There are finitely many slow endpoints there. Consequently
some positive-length closed interval `J` inside `(0,4)` is strictly safe for
every limiting slow color. Shrink J if needed. Endpoint continuity makes
the same J safe for all slow colors sufficiently far along the sequence.

The fast blocked union has measure in J at most

`|F| |J|/4 + (3/16)sum_(i in F) p_i`,

which is strictly below `|J|` eventually. Thus J contains a safe point for
all four colors. A chain starting at zero cannot cross that point, so all
its selected endpoints then lie in `[0,4]`.

For every slow color, eventually `p_i>=p_i*/2`; it can have at most
`floor(8/p_i*)+1` selected endpoints in that range. Let K be the sum of these
finite bounds over the slow colors. Between their at most K occurrences,
there are at most K+1 fast-only chain pieces. The preceding three-color
bound gives

`N <= K+15(K+1)`.

Hence an unbounded sequence of normalized four-color chain counts cannot
have a subsequence with vanishing periods. This is a boundary-exclusion
lemma, not an explicit global four-color bound: K depends on the positive
limiting periods. The all-positive limiting case, recurrence needed to turn
a limiting half-line cover into a critical tiling, and the complete tiling
classification require separate arguments.
